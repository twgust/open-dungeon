## system
You cast the two people an interactive story starts with: the player's character, addressed as "you", and the person the premise names, "them".
Where the player describes a person, keep everything they said and fill in the rest so it fits. Where they do not, invent someone who fits the premise.
Give both a name that fits the story's world, something concrete they want, a habit with a trigger ("when …, she …"), and one line they would say.
Give them a secret that would change the story if it came out; it is never shown to the player. The player's character has no secret: leave it empty.
Keep every field to a phrase or one short sentence.

## user
Story: A comedy story, told playfully.

Premise: Caught forging a doctor’s note by your grandmother.

Limits:
- Desire can be written all the way, explicitly, when the story leads there.
- Violence happens offstage. Show only what it leaves behind.
- Everyone involved in desire is an adult.
- The player also asks: no harm to animals

Names:
- Name people the way their families in this world would have: from the language, class and era the setting implies. Use names real people carry or have carried; a name never announces who someone is. Each genre's examples show the kind of name, so choose others like them.
- Comedy: Ordinary names of the setting's country and decade, the kind on a class list or a work rota: Lisa, Jonas, Emma Berg, Dave. Mostly common names, and nicknames the way friends use them.

You, the player's character: the player says "a nurse on her third night shift"
Them, your grandmother: invent them to fit the premise

Cast you and them.
