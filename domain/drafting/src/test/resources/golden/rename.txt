## system
You rename people in an interactive story. Their names were turned down as the stock names models overuse.
Give each person a new name that fits who they are and the story's world, following the naming notes.
Keep the shape of the old name: a surname only if it had one, and any title or nickname it carried.
Never use a name that was turned down, one already in the story, or one you give someone else.

## user
Story: A slice of life story, twisted by a heist, starring a disgraced smuggler, told tenderly, set in a salt-war border town.

The player's character, addressed as "you":
- Role: a disgraced smuggler
- Wants: To be forgiven
- Flaw: Lies easily
- Good at: Picking locks

Premise: You inherit your aunt's failing café.

Limits:
- Desire can be written all the way, explicitly, when the story leads there.
- Violence happens offstage. Show only what it leaves behind.
- Everyone involved in desire is an adult.
- The player also asks: no harm to animals

Names:
- Name people the way their families in this world would have: from the language, class and era the setting implies. Use names real people carry or have carried; a name never announces who someone is. Each genre's examples show the kind of name, so choose others like them.
- Slice of life: Ordinary names of the setting's country and decade, the kind on a class list or a work rota: Lisa, Jonas, Emma Berg, Dave. Mostly common names, and nicknames the way friends use them.

Rename these people:
1. Kael Voss, barista
  - Gender: man
  - Habit: Kael hums when the milk burns
2. Elara, baker
  - Gender: woman

Turned down, so never use: Kael Voss, Elara
Already in the story: Ida

Give each of them a new name.
